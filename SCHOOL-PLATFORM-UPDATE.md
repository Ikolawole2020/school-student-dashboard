# Starlight Model School — School Platform Update

**Empowering minds, shaping futures.**

A summary of what is new on the school platform, what it means for each
member of our community, and where we are heading next.

---

## Dear Parents, Teachers and Students,

Over the past few weeks the school platform has been rebuilt and extended. This
update explains, in plain language, what has changed, why we made each change,
and how it will benefit your child and our school.

Some of this work is visible at a glance. Other parts — particularly the
protection of student data — are less visible but, in our view, just as
important. We have tried to cover both.

---

## 1. Attendance is now recorded every school day

**What it does**

Teachers can now mark a daily register directly from the platform. For each
student there are four possible marks: **Present**, **Absent**, **Late**, or
**Excused**, with an optional note explaining an unusual mark.

**Why it matters**

Attendance is one of the strongest indicators of a child's progress. A pattern
of lateness or absence is often an early signal that a student needs support,
and it is far easier to act on when a teacher can see the whole picture rather
than relying on memory.

**What it brings to the school**

- Teachers save the time previously spent writing and retyping registers
- Patterns of absence become visible early, so support can be offered sooner
- Students see their own attendance record and percentage
- The school gains accurate attendance data for its own reporting

**Made practical for busy teachers**

Two features were included specifically because marking thirty students by hand
is slow. A teacher can tap **"All present"** and then change only the few
students who are not there. The previous day's register also loads as a
starting point. If a register is marked in error, the whole day can be cleared
and redone in one action. A student who is not selected keeps their existing
mark, so correcting one pupil never disturbs the rest of the class.

## 2. A Holiday Calendar

**What it does**

Public holidays and school closures are recorded in one shared calendar, each
with a date and a short description.

**Why it matters**

A calendar that says "Independence Day — school closed all day" removes a great
deal of confusion for families, and prevents the situation where a teacher
marks an entire register of absences on a day the school was actually shut.

**What it brings to the school**

- Parents and staff always know the next closure and why
- The attendance page automatically warns a teacher before they mark a register
  on a holiday
- No more registers filled with absences on a public holiday

---

## 3. An Assignment Hub with Image Submission

**What it does**

Teachers post assignments — with a title, subject, class, due date and
instructions — and may attach photographs of questions, diagrams or scanned
sheets. Students open the assignment, read the instructions, and **upload
photographs of their own written work** as their answer.

**Why it matters**

This addresses a real limitation of a digital platform. Not every child has
reliable internet access at home, and a child working in an exercise book cannot
"submit" it. Being able to photograph the work and upload it removes that
barrier entirely — the teacher receives the real work, exactly as written, in
the child's own hand.

**What it brings to the school**

- Students who would otherwise be disadvantaged by poor connectivity can still
  complete and submit work
- Teachers receive clearer, more complete work than a typed answer alone
- Nothing is lost: a student may upload several images, and may re-upload if
  they realise they photographed the wrong page
- Teachers record scores against each assignment and give written feedback

**Teachers can be selective about grading.** As with exam results, a teacher
may enter marks for only the students who actually submitted. A blank entry is
never treated as a zero, so a student who missed one assignment is not
unfairly penalised.

## 4. Weekly Lesson Plans

**What it does**

Teachers record their weekly lesson plans — objectives, activities, resources
and homework — and may attach supporting images such as diagrams and worksheets.

**Why it matters**

Lesson plans are among the most valuable documents a school produces. Storing
them digitally means they are no longer lost on a desk or in a cupboard, can be
reviewed by a head of department or the principal, and can be picked up smoothly
by another teacher if a class needs cover.

**What it brings to the school**

- A clear, searchable record of what has been taught each week
- Better continuity when a teacher is absent
- A basis for reviewing the curriculum across the school
- Practical support material kept alongside the plan

Each teacher manages their own lesson plans. School administrators can view
every plan across the school, which makes supervision and quality assurance far
more straightforward.


---

## 5. News, Announcements and Testimonials

**What it does**

The school can now publish news, announcements, events and achievements on the
public website, with photographs, and can publish testimonials from parents and
students.

**Why it matters**

Previously the front page was a fixed design that could only be changed by
someone who could edit the website code. That meant school news was rare, slow,
and out of date. Now the school office can publish an item the same day.

**What it brings to the school**

- Parents hear about events, results and achievements as they happen
- The public page reflects the life of the school rather than a static brochure
- Testimonials give prospective parents a genuine sense of the school's
  character, written by the community rather than by the school about itself

Posts can be written in advance and **saved as a draft** before they go live, so
nothing appears publicly by accident. Once published, a post can be hidden or
removed at any time.

## 6. Results entered the way teachers actually work

**Why it matters**

Marking is now more forgiving in a way that is easy to overlook but very
important. A student who was unwell and sat only some of the examinations is no
longer disadvantaged — their results are recorded for the subjects they did
complete.

Marking is also now checked for errors. A mark that would produce a score above
the maximum is rejected with a clear explanation, so an accidental keypress
cannot quietly distort a child's record.

**Promotion has also been made safer.** Previously a student could be moved up a
class on the strength of a single subject being entered. A student is now
promoted at the end of term only when **every** subject for that term has been
recorded. Partial entry remains fully supported — it simply no longer triggers a
promotion on its own.

## 7. Student Privacy

**Why this is on the list**

Result checking used to accept any student ID typed into the form. With student
IDs being short and based on names, this meant that any signed-in user could, in
principle, view another child's results.

**What has changed**

- A student can now see **only their own** results
- A teacher or administrator may look up a student they are responsible for
- Passwords are stored only as scrambled hashes. Nobody, including the school
  office, can read a student's or teacher's password
- Accounts issued by the school must have their password changed at first login,
  so a temporary password never becomes a permanent one

We raise this because protecting a child's academic record is not a technical
detail. It is a matter of trust, and parents are entitled to know how their
child's information is handled.

## 8. A modern, easier-to-use design

The entire platform has been redesigned. The most useful changes are not
cosmetic:

**It works properly on a phone.** The menus, forms, tables and buttons now
rearrange correctly on small screens. This matters more than it sounds: a
parent checking results from a phone during their lunch break is a realistic
scenario, and it previously did not work well.

**A light and dark theme.** The platform follows the setting of the phone or
computer automatically, and can be changed with a single tap. Reading a results
table in bright sunlight is far easier in light mode; working at night is far
easier in dark mode.

**Clearer feedback.** When something is saved the platform confirms it
immediately in the corner of the screen, rather than pushing the page content
downwards with a large coloured banner. If something needs attention, it is
explained.

**Faster to use.** Filters and search now work across students, staff, classes
and assignments. Forms show exactly what is required, and errors are explained
rather than merely rejected.

**Accessible by design.** Larger tap targets, clear text contrast, full keyboard
support, and a skip-to-content option for screen readers.

## 9. Simpler staff records

Adding a member of staff now requires only three details: **name, phone number
and class**. The staff identification number, email address and an opening
password are generated automatically.

Class is optional, because not every member of staff manages a class — the
bursary, kitchen and administrative staff do not. A class can be assigned later
whenever it is appropriate.

The school office can view every staff login from one page, which means a
teacher who has forgotten their password can be helped in a moment rather than
locked out.

---

## 10. Contact details for families

A **guardian's phone number** can now be recorded for every student — on
registration, when editing a record, and by the student themselves.

Keeping this accurate matters. It means the school can reach a family quickly in
an emergency, and it removes one of the most common reasons a call to the school
office ends without reaching anyone.

The platform can also search its records by phone number.

## What happens next

We intend to continue building on the platform in the following order of
priority:

1. **Fee and payment records** — so parents can see what is owed and what has
   been paid
2. **Report cards** — a printable version of a child's results, position in
   class and teacher's remarks
3. **A mobile-friendly parent view** — seeing attendance, results and school
   notices in one place
4. **Improved account security** — allowing students and parents to set their
   own passwords rather than using one issued by the school

---

## A note on privacy and data

Student information is among the most sensitive material the school holds. We
have taken steps to ensure that:

- No database file, and no backup of one, is stored in the public code repository
- The repository is configured so that student records and uploaded photographs
  can never be added to it by accident
- Passwords are scrambled, so they cannot be read even by someone with access to
  the school's files

We will continue to review how student data is handled as the platform grows.
